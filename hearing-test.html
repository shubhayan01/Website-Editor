Do You Need a Test?

Signs You May Have Hearing Loss

If any of these sound familiar, a hearing evaluation is a simple, free first step.

🗣️

Asking People to Repeat

Frequently asking friends, family, or colleagues to repeat themselves — especially on the phone.

📺

TV Volume Too High

Others in the room complain your TV or radio is too loud for them.

🍽️

Struggling in Restaurants

Difficulty following conversations in noisy environments like restaurants or social gatherings.

📞

Phone Conversations Are Hard

You need to concentrate harder on phone calls and still miss parts of what's said.

🔔

Missing Sounds

You no longer hear sounds you used to — birds, doorbells, or someone calling from another room.

😴

Exhaustion from Listening

Feeling mentally tired at the end of the day from the effort of concentrating to hear and understand.

The Process

What to Expect at Your Evaluation

Our hearing evaluations typically take 45–60 minutes and are completely free with no obligation.

1

Case History

We start by learning about your hearing history, lifestyle, and the specific situations where you notice difficulty.

2

Visual Ear Inspection

A brief otoscopic exam to check the ear canal and eardrum — rules out blockages or other physical causes.

3

Pure-Tone Audiometry

You'll listen through headphones and respond to a series of tones across frequencies. Painless and straightforward.

4

Speech Testing

We assess how well you understand speech at different volumes to determine your real-world hearing ability.

5

Results & Recommendations

We walk you through your audiogram, explain what it means, and only recommend a hearing aid if you genuinely need one.

👂
Free & No Obligation

Our evaluations are 100% free. There is never any pressure to purchase — we believe you deserve to understand your hearing before making any decision.

Types of Tests

Tests We Perform

Pure-Tone Audiometry

The standard hearing test — you listen to tones at different pitches and volumes to map your hearing thresholds across the full frequency range.

Speech Audiometry

Tests how well you understand words and sentences at various volumes, reflecting real-world listening challenges.

Tympanometry

Measures how well your eardrum moves in response to pressure changes — helps identify middle ear conditions like fluid or perforations.

Real-Ear Measurement

Used when fitting hearing aids — confirms the device is delivering the precise amplification prescribed for your individual ear canal.
